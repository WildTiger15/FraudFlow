# Risks and controls

| Risk | What could go wrong | How FraudFlow addresses it | What a real deployment would still need |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Privacy** | Customer data sent to an AI service or stored insecurely; data used beyond its purpose | Synthetic PaySim data only; no real data is used, requested or simulated. The drafter receives only the minimum facts (transactions for two accounts, no names or identity). Secrets come from environment variables; `.env` is gitignored. | Privacy impact assessment; contract and data residency terms for the AI provider (no training on bank data, retention limits); role based access; encryption; redaction of identifiers. |
| **AI errors and hallucination** | The draft states an amount, date or transaction that is not in the data, or a wrong conclusion | The model is instructed to use only the given facts, cite transaction IDs and say "not enough information". `agent/checks.py` verifies every number and ID against the facts and that the flagged transaction is cited; failures are labelled LOW CONFIDENCE. The reviewer sees the source transactions next to the draft. Tests prove invented IDs and numbers are caught. | Ongoing quality sampling by a second reviewer; tracking of edit and reject reasons; checks for wrong *interpretation*, which number matching cannot catch. |
| **Bias in flagging** | Rules or models flag some customer groups more often, for reasons unrelated to risk | Rules are simple and explainable, with written reasons and thresholds in one place. No demographic data is used. Per-rule alert volumes and precision are reported, so a noisy rule is visible. | Fairness testing on real (protected) attributes under governance; review of thresholds over time; documented rationale for each scenario. |
| **Over reliance on AI** | Reviewers rubber stamp drafts, especially under backlog pressure | The AI never decides: every case needs a named person's approve, edit or reject. Drafts are labelled as suggestions and LOW CONFIDENCE ones stand out. The investigator guide says when to reject. The timing study records how often drafts are approved without edits. | Monitoring of approval rates and review times per reviewer (very fast approvals are a warning sign); training; periodic manual-only reviews to keep skills. |
| **Regulatory and audit needs** | Cannot show regulators why a case was escalated or closed; AI decides something it should not | Every alert stores its reasons. Every case stores the exact facts given to the AI, the draft, the model name, check results and confidence. Every decision stores reviewer, time and channel, once per case. FraudFlow never files a UTR or STR or contacts anyone. | Model risk management (validation, documentation, change control); retention per FINTRAC record keeping rules; immutable audit storage; sign-off by the AML compliance officer. |
| **Detection gaps** | Thresholds tuned to one dataset miss new patterns or overload the team | Detection numbers include sample sizes, and the one threshold revision is documented with the caveat that the labels were visible (`docs/DETECTION_NOTES.md`). Anomaly detection runs alongside rules. | Held-out evaluation; regular below-the-line testing; tuning governance. |
| **Security of the flow** | Anyone could read cases or post fake decisions through the API | Optional shared `X-API-Key`; one decision per case; inputs validated; the public demo is read-only. | Proper authentication (Entra ID / OAuth), network restrictions, no anonymous tunnels. |
